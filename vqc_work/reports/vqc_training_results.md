## Preprocessing and encoding
StandardScaler (fit: 6,000 training rows) -> PCA(4, svd_solver="full", random_state=42; fit: scaled training rows) -> angle encoding. Validation rows are only transformed.

| component | explained variance ratio | cumulative | angle scale (pi / max train abs) | direct angle range (train) | scaled angle range (val) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PC1 | 0.4794 | 0.4794 | 0.3658 | [-8.59, 4.37] | [-2.95, 1.62] |
| PC2 | 0.2136 | 0.6930 | 0.4035 | [-5.85, 7.79] | [-2.25, 2.93] |
| PC3 | 0.0703 | 0.7632 | 0.4795 | [-6.55, 4.83] | [-2.86, 2.73] |
| PC4 | 0.0496 | 0.8128 | 0.7642 | [-4.11, 3.74] | [-3.07, 2.89] |

- **Why the encoding was changed.** The originally specified *direct* encoding feeds raw PCA scores into `RY`. Those scores span about +-8.6 rad, and 52.8% of training rows have at least one angle outside [-pi, pi]; because `RY` is 2pi-periodic, distant samples wrap onto similar rotations. The *scaled* encoding multiplies each component by pi / max|train score| (fitted on train only), keeping the map one-to-one on the training range. This change was made explicitly, approved by the team, and the direct encoding is still trained and reported as an ablation. Validation rows outside [-pi, pi] after scaling: 0.00%.
- 4 components keep 81.3% of the scaled-feature variance. An audit diagnostic run outside this script found that a logistic regression on the same 4 training-fitted components also classifies the validation set perfectly, so the 4-component bottleneck does not remove the class information; remaining errors come from the quantum model.

## Circuit (verified against the implementation)
- 4 qubits, `RY(angle_i)` on qubit i, then per layer: trainable `RY` x4, trainable `RZ` x4, CX sequence [(0, 1), (1, 0), (1, 2), (2, 1), (2, 3), (3, 2), (3, 0), (0, 3)]. Readout `z = <Z_0>`, `logit = w z + b`, `p = sigmoid(logit)`, fixed threshold 0.5.
- Nominal trainable parameters: 8 per layer + 2 readout (1 layer: 10, 2 layers: 18). Logical resources: 1 layer depth 11, CX 8; 2 layers depth 21, CX 16.
- **Structural finding (gradient-verified).** The final CX ring maps `Z_0` back to `Z_0 Z_2 Z_3` (verified numerically, max error 1.2e-16). Qubit 1 therefore never reaches the readout through the last layer, and `RZ` gates directly before the last ring cannot change a Z-basis outcome. Parameters with exactly zero gradient at every tested point:

| layers | quantum params | dead | effective | dead parameters | mean |d<Z0>/d angle| for PC1..PC4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 | 5 | 3 | layer0_RY_q1, layer0_RZ_q0, layer0_RZ_q1, layer0_RZ_q2, layer0_RZ_q3 | 0.251, 0.000, 0.248, 0.275 |
| 2 | 16 | 6 | 10 | layer0_RZ_q2, layer1_RY_q1, layer1_RZ_q0, layer1_RZ_q1, layer1_RZ_q2, layer1_RZ_q3 | 0.081, 0.101, 0.089, 0.088 |

  Consequence: the 1-layer model **ignores PC2 entirely** and its output is a product of cosines of PC1, PC3 and PC4; the 2-layer model uses all four inputs. The architecture is kept as specified (dead parameters are harmless for training, they simply never move); removing them would change the specified ansatz.

## Training protocol
Adam (PennyLane `AdamOptimizer`), BCE with logits, batch 16 (375 batches per epoch), 30 epochs, seed 42 for initialisation and batch order, parameters initialised N(0, 0.1^2), readout w = 1, b = 0. Gradients: analytic backpropagation through the statevector simulator. The predeclared grid (layers {1, 2} x lr {0.01, 0.001}) is run for both encodings (231 s wall, 8 runs in parallel). Selection per encoding: highest validation macro-F1, ties -> fewer layers, lr 0.001, earlier epoch.

## Comparison with the classical baseline (same data, validation-tuned)
Logistic regression on all 30 standardised features (teammate's grid, read-only from `reports/classical_baseline_metrics.csv`): validation macro-F1 1.0000 (10 of 10 grid points at this value); every validation sample is correct, so its bootstrap interval is [1.0000, 1.0000].
The canonical VQC reaches 0.9993 (1 of 1500 validation samples wrong, 95% CI [0.9979, 1.0000]) from 4 PCA inputs and 18 nominal parameters, under ideal simulation only. It does not beat the classical baseline, and no quantum advantage is claimed: the classical model is simpler, exact and noise-free, while the VQC's validation macro-F1 under the official noise model is 0.4663 (mean of 5 simulator seeds).

## Limitations
- Validation is used for selection, so validation scores are optimistic; there is no held-out estimate inside this workstream.
- The predeclared search is small; one canonical seed is reported, with four extra seeds for robustness.
- The specified ansatz contains structurally dead parameters (see above) and its 1-layer version cannot see PC2.
- The scaled encoding was introduced after inspecting the direct encoding's behaviour; that design step is itself informed by validation results and is disclosed as such.
- Exact (shot-free) ideal results; noisy results include shot noise.
- No claim of quantum advantage or clinical validity; the data are synthetic statistics modelled on MIT-BIH.
- Process note: earlier in the project session a shell row-count (`wc -l` over a glob of the q1 CSVs) touched the unlabelled test-features file; no content was read or used, and this script has no code path to that file (asserted by `--selftest`).
